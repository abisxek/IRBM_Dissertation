Abstract
Spatial proteomics is essential for mapping the tumor microenvironment in glioma. However, physical assays remain slow and expensive, while generating substantial biohazardous plastic waste that cannot be recycled due to cancer cell exposure risks. Computational approaches offer a promising alternative, yet current methods address this challenge only in fragments. Graph attention networks (GATs) have demonstrated strong spatial RNA-to-protein predictions across tissue types, dense vision-pathology models can infer gene expression directly from histology images down to single-cell resolutions, biologically informed neural networks (BINNs) using pathway constraints routinely outperform legacy baselines like XGBoost on proteomics tasks, and multi-omics foundation models are beginning to learn cross-modal RNA-to-protein translation at scale.

Despite these advances, no single solution unifies spatial topology, pathway-guided gene selection, and histology fusion within a framework built for the operational realities of a translational glioma lab, where compute budgets, turnaround time, and interpretability matter just as much as raw accuracy. To bridge this gap, we present a graph-based, cross-modal framework that generates accurate virtual protein maps directly from spatial transcriptomics. By leveraging GATs, our model captures spatial neighborhood topologies while learning complex, non-linear relationships between gene transcripts and protein synthesis.

Rather than committing to a single static architecture, we systematically evaluate four complementary strategies:

1) Pathway-Guided Selection: Embedding curated genetic pathway databases (in the spirit of BINNs) to boost biological interpretability.

2) Targeted Gene Filtering: A streamlined pathway variant designed to maintain performance using a fraction of the input features.

3) Truncated SVD: A computationally lightweight alternative for fast dimensionality reduction.

4) Histology Feature Fusion: Integrating ResNet-extracted tissue image features to resolve fine-grained structures like 20 µm immune checkpoints that spot-level methods tend to blur.

From this experimental space, we construct and benchmark three distinct final pipelines: one maximized strictly for predictive accuracy, one optimized for rapid high-throughput processing, and one balanced middle ground. By carrying all three forward, our framework directly addresses real-world laboratory trade-offs, delivering a validated, low-waste path to virtual proteomics in glioma research.
